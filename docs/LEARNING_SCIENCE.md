# Learning Science and Evidence Plan v0.1

This is an evidence-informed design, **not a claim that each technique works for every person**.

## Principles
- **Retrieval practice / testing effect:** ask learners to produce the answer without showing the mnemonic before feedback.
- **Spacing:** schedule delayed recall according to measured history; FSRS is a candidate scheduler, not a guarantee.
- **Keyword mnemonics:** learner-chosen phonetic cues and vivid imagery may assist initial word learning; they can also mislead pronunciation or fail to transfer to natural speech.
- **Dual coding / multimedia:** support verbal and visual cues when relevant; remove unnecessary visual complexity.
- **Cognitive load:** constrain simultaneous novelty; provide optional scaffolds and gradual fading.
- **Contextualized practice and transfer:** use words in varied new sentences and action contexts.
- **Interleaving:** alternate confusable genders, meanings and forms after foundations are learned.
- **Feedback and metacognition:** show corrected forms, confidence, actual recall and common errors.
- **Generation effect:** let the learner construct mnemonic imagery instead of assuming an AI-generated one is superior.
- **Accessibility:** provide no-visual and no-audio paths, subtitles, keyboard controls.

## Claims requiring experimentation
- More bizarre or more objects in mnemonic scenes is **not necessarily** better.
- VR may provide embodiment and situational context but is **not assumed** to beat efficient 2D recall exercises.
- Pronunciation scoring from ASR is not equivalent to verified phonetic assessment.
- Do not categorize users into fixed visual/auditory/kinesthetic learning styles; these profiles lack reliable evidence as prescriptive teaching types.

## Experimental protocol
Pilot 100–300 words stratified by frequency, concreteness, part of speech and prior knowledge. Within-learner counterbalanced comparison:
(A) translation + retrieval,
(B) suggested mnemonic,
(C) self-made mnemonic,
(D) collaboratively constructed mnemonic.
Randomize across matched word sets, avoid prior-known items, and balance item difficulty. Record time-on-task and total resource cost.

Outcomes: unaided German recall from Russian and reverse, article accuracy, delayed retention at 1/7/30 days, sentence transfer, pronunciation review, subjective workload and accessibility. Compare conditions while controlling initial knowledge and practice duration; report uncertainty, attrition, multiple comparisons and whether differences are practically meaningful.

Only adapt from reliable observations; don't optimize solely for right-now accuracy or engagement. Provide opt-out of experiments.

## Mathematical methods
- Probabilistic state estimation for mastery and uncertainty.
- Time-to-forgetting / survival modeling and spaced scheduling.
- Bayesian hierarchical models for learner/item effects, with conservative cold starts.
- Graphs linking form, sense, pronunciation, associations and scene objects.
- Constraint satisfaction and optimization under time/cost limits.
- Finite-state grammars or dependency constraints for safe elementary scene generation.
- Spatial geometry and physics engines for XR manipulation.

## Initial references to verify in a formal evidence review
Classic work by Atkinson & Raugh on keyword mnemonics, Paivio on imagery/verbal coding, Roediger & Karpicke on retrieval, Cepeda et al. on spacing, Sweller on cognitive load, and more recent meta-analyses of L2 vocabulary mnemonics, embodiment and VR learning. Verify original publications, effect sizes, participant populations and licensing before presenting efficacy claims as settled.
